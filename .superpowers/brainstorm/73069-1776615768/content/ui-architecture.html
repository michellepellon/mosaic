Mosaic: Information Architecture

The current dashboard mixes 4 levels of information on one scrolling page. A coherent UX progressively discloses depth.

The Problem

Current Layout (one endless scroll)
Daily Brief (readiness score)
Protocol Scorecard (10 metrics table)
Clinical Biomarkers (lab results table)
Trends (9 small multiple charts)
Gait & Mobility (2 charts)
Correlations (3 dual-axis charts)
Cardiovascular (2 charts)
Sleep Architecture (2 charts)
SQL Console
~15 sections, no hierarchy, no narrative

Which redesign direction?

A

Two-Mode: Brief + Lab

Brief is the landing page: readiness score, prescription, key numbers, alerts. Nothing else. One screen, no scrolling.

Lab is the analysis workspace: tabbed by domain (Recovery, Physical, Metabolic, Custom). Each tab has relevant charts + an inline SQL console. Click a tab to explore that domain.

The morning check takes 10 seconds. Deep analysis is one click away.

Pros

  • Clear separation of intent
  • Brief stays focused
  • Domains organize the chaos

Cons

  • Tab switching adds friction
  • Harder to see cross-domain patterns
B

Progressive Disclosure: Stack with Collapse

Brief at top (always visible). Below it, collapsible domain cards: Recovery, Physical, Metabolic. Each card shows a 1-line summary when collapsed ("Recovery: 82/100 — sleep excellent, HRV below baseline"). Click to expand and see the charts.

Console pinned at the bottom, always accessible.

Everything on one page, but only the brief demands attention. You expand what you need.

Pros

  • Single page, no mode switching
  • Domain summaries give overview
  • Drill-down on demand

Cons

  • Collapsed cards still take space
  • Risk of "accordion fatigue"
C

Command Center: Brief + Grid

Brief as a top banner (compact: score, status, prescription in one row). Below it, a dense grid of small cards — each card is one metric with a sparkline and current value. Click any card to expand into a full analysis view with charts, correlations, and SQL.

Console accessible via a card or keyboard shortcut.

Like a flight deck — everything visible at a glance, drill into anything.

Pros

  • Dense overview for power users
  • Every metric is one click from deep analysis
  • Feels like a performance center

Cons

  • More complex to build
  • Grid layout harder to make responsive
  • Lots of small cards can feel noisy
